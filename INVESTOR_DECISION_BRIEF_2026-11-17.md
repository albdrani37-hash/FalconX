## Investment case in 60 seconds

AI-agent teams can report benchmark scores, release results, and test outcomes without being able to account precisely for every source record, exclusion, denominator, derived result, or unresolved evidence gap. **FalconXAI proposes an evidence-reconciliation workflow between agent traces and claims made about their performance.**

The immediate route to market is a *bounded, paid AI-agent evaluation-evidence review*: an engineering team supplies an authorized frozen evaluation export and explicit review questions; FalconXAI produces a reproducible account of record inclusion/exclusion, lineage, inconsistencies, and supported versus unresolved claims. Recurrent paid problems could justify reusable adapters and a productized assurance workflow. This is a **commercialization hypothesis**, not a proven recurring software business.

**What is already real:** authored public software and dated research artifacts, frozen-corpus accounting, synthetic fault tests, a separate Python/SQLite QA demonstration, and documentation of failure modes. **What is not yet proven:** customer demand, production utility, independent replication, pricing acceptance, revenue, or product-market fit.

## Buyer and initial purchase decision

**Potential budget owner:** AI-product engineering lead, evaluation lead, assurance-services provider, or an enterprise owner of an agent release decision. Actual qualified paying buyer accounts are not yet established.

**First purchasable outcome:** a contracted, scoped evidence-review package with reproducible checks, source-count reconciliation, a machine-readable findings register, limitations, and an acceptance report. Existing nonbinding service options include a diagnostic, evidence review, and a separately scoped custom subcontract. These are **proposed fees**, not validated pricing or booked revenue.

**Qualification gate before delivery:** named buyer and purchasing authority, approved data rights, a frozen data/sample definition, a budget, independent-contractor eligibility, a secure payment path, explicit acceptance criteria, and written scope. No uncontrolled production access or undocumented safety certification.

**Potential differentiation to test:** evidence-population reconciliation and claim lineage alongside, not in place of, established observability/evaluation platforms. FalconXAI does not claim superiority over LangSmith, Langfuse, Arize Phoenix, or enterprise assurance providers.

## Diligence items and explicit risk register

1. **Customer-demand risk — high.** No independently verified paying enterprise customer, LOI, production rollout, software subscriptions or collected recurring revenue. First improvement is a paid buyer scope or independent demand confirmation.
2. **Team/key-person risk — high.** Solo founder. Future commercial delivery, security, customer success and sales capacity are unproven; proposed hiring is not a current team.
3. **Legal and finance readiness — high.** The initiative is pre-incorporation. A company, validated cap table, legal rights chain, appropriate contracts, applicable taxation, and investment-acceptance structure remain to be established before equity investment.
4. **Technical generalization risk — high.** Reference experiments are benchmark-specific and partly synthetic; no external replication, customer production or standards certification claimed.
5. **Market/pricing risk — high.** No validated reachable SAM, buying cycle, repeat purchase, price acceptance or contribution-margin data. Adjacent AI-governance market forecasts are not FalconXAI revenue.
6. **IP chain of title.** Authorship, dated releases and open-source licenses support technical diligence but do not confer exclusive ownership over all upstream components. A later entity must document assignment/licensing rights.

**Next independent diligence decision:** run the public reviewer example, examine fault-localization misses, verify technical authorship/rights, and review a buyer-approved pilot scope before underwriting meaningful scale assumptions.
